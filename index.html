The beginning

Every journey starts small. Mine began at a food bank and a shoreline — groceries one week, trash bags the next. I didn't plan a path. I just kept noticing things.

How much a shoreline can hold. How far a meal can travel. How a place leaves its mark on a body. This page is what I noticed.

Coconut Creek, Florida · Sea level · Grades 9–12

The shore was my first classroom.

Fort Lauderdale Beach Cleanup

What the tide leaves behind

I picked up litter, sorted recyclables, and talked with visitors about why the shoreline matters. Some mornings the tide brought back more than we carried away — and I started asking where it all comes from.

Coral Springs Food Distribution

Food that almost wasn't

With local supermarkets, I helped collect near-expiry groceries and redistribute them to families in need. A carton of milk pulled from a dumpster-bound pallet could still end up on someone's table — a small victory worth repeating.

Berkeley, California · Pre-college summer session · Grade 10

From shoreline to syllabus.

During the 6-week UC Berkeley summer session, I enrolled in EPS 80 Environmental Earth Science and COLWRIT 9C Multilingual Academic Writing — my first chance to give the beach its proper language.

I earned an A in both courses, achieving a 4.0 term GPA. This summer experience deepened my understanding of how Earth systems interact with human health — laying the academic foundation for my later fieldwork on environmental and public health.

For the first time, the beach and the syllabus felt like the same place.

Shihezi, Xinjiang & Yushu, Qinghai · 450 m → 3,700 m · Grades 10–11

On the roof of the world, I watched the environment write itself on the body.

With the "Eyes Care Action" program, I helped screen children in remote communities: 4,000 across 38 schools in Shihezi, Xinjiang (2025), and 5,000 across 8 schools in Yushu and Nangqian, Qinghai (2026).

The numbers told a story the textbooks hadn't: in these high, bright valleys, intense ultraviolet light and thin, dry air take a visible toll on children's eyes. I had come to help — and I ended up learning why the help was needed.

I recorded eye-health data and taught children how to protect their sight.

That same summer, back in Beijing, I joined Roots & Shoots' YACC (Youth Action for Climate Change) youth green program — the plateau had taught me what the environment does to a body; YACC was my turn to ask what young people can do for the environment.

In Yushu, a reincarnated lama came to the screening line, and monks helped translate and organize the day. Care only works if it speaks the community's language — and respects its altitude. Hoson Foundation ↗

iGEM · Targeted therapy for non-small cell lung cancer · Grade 11

The air we breathe reaches the cells we lose.

In Grade 11, I led the wiki team of the ZhuLu China iGEM project — engineering RGD-xES, a fusion protein that attacks non-small-cell lung cancer physically instead of chemically. Its RGD module locks onto integrins on the tumor surface; its elastin-like ES repeats then self-assemble under the tumor's acidic microenvironment, capping the cell in a protein shell.

Across five design–build–test–learn cycles, the candidates RGD-6ES and RGD-8ES left normal bronchial cells near 100% viable at 1 mg/mL over seven days — yet at half that dose cut A549 lung-cancer viability to roughly a third, and under a tenth, within 48 hours. The environment I had been reading outdoors was suddenly legible in a petri dish: pH, hypoxia, the geography of a tumor.

The same thread — place enters the body — ran from a snowfield to a petri dish. View Full iGEM Wiki ↗

Hero EMR · Medical informatization internship · Grade 11

Healing the system that heals.

As a paid summer intern at Hero EMR — a startup using AI to help U.S. small clinics run at lower cost — I studied how China's medical system manages high patient volume.

I analyzed China's health platforms and hospital information systems, and turned the full patient journey — registration to settlement — into concrete product suggestions for Hero EMR.

I walked through hospital lobbies in Beijing mapping patient flows the way I had once walked a beach mapping litter. The system people move through is an environment too — and it can be redesigned.

Visit heroemr.com ↗

Geriatric nutrition & health · Back to the table · Grades 10–12

The journey returns to the table.

At the 2025 Geriatric Nutrition & Health Conference, I sat in on large-scale cohort research — studies tracking thousands of older adults to ask what they eat, what they lack, what fails first.

I'm now contributing to a nutrition-science paper, and my IB Economics Extended Essay asks whether China's senior food standards can ease the "lemon market" — whether regulation can protect older consumers when quality is hard to see.

The groceries I once recovered and the cohort studies I now follow are asking the same question from two ends of the same table.
